# Athanase Papadopoulos: math.HO and Teichmüller-history bridges

Athanase Papadopoulos's historical papers belong here because Teichmüller theory is not only a metric on a moduli space. Its development runs through Riemann surfaces, uniformization, quasiconformal maps, Fuchsian/Kleinian groups, measured foliations and laminations, mapping class groups, and Thurston's asymmetric geometry.

This file is a **cross-reference and reading guide**. It links to legitimate public source pages; it does not copy paper PDFs into the repository.

## Direct Teichmüller / moduli history

- **Norbert A'Campo, Lizhen Ji, Athanase Papadopoulos, “On the early history of moduli and Teichmüller spaces.”** arXiv:1602.07208, math.HO / math.CV / math.GT.
  https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.07208
  Best historical spine for this repository: Riemann surfaces → moduli → uniformization/Fuchsian groups → Teichmüller → Ahlfors/Bers, with Klein, Poincaré, Weil, Grothendieck and others along the chain.

- **Lizhen Ji, Athanase Papadopoulos, “Historical development of Teichmüller theory.”** *Archive for History of Exact Sciences* 67 (2013), 119–147.
  https://doi.org/10.1007/s00407-012-0104-y
  Especially useful for seeing why mapping-class-group, hyperbolic, analytic and moduli viewpoints should not be isolated from one another.

## Quasiconformal ancestry

- **Athanase Papadopoulos, “Nicolas-Auguste Tissot: A link between cartography and quasiconformal theory.”** arXiv:1612.00279, math.HO.
  https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.00279

- **Athanase Papadopoulos, “Quasiconformal mappings, from Ptolemy's geography to the work of Teichmüller.”** arXiv:1702.03756, math.HO / math.CV / math.DG / math.GT.
  https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.03756

- **Athanase Papadopoulos, “A note on Nicolas-Auguste Tissot: At the origin of quasiconformal mappings.”** arXiv:2001.03434, math.HO / math.CV / math.DG.
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.03434

The Tissot indicatrix is worth remembering computationally: infinitesimal circles become ellipses, so angular and metric distortion become visual objects. That gives a concrete bridge from old cartography to quasiconformal maps and Teichmüller extremal problems.

## Teichmüller beyond the usual moduli-space story

- **Athanase Papadopoulos, “Value distribution theory and Teichmüller's paper ‘Einfache Beispiele zur Wertverteilungslehre’.”** arXiv:2001.10536, math.HO / math.CV.
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.10536
  Useful corrective to treating Teichmüller only as “the Teichmüller-space person”: this follows his work into value-distribution theory and its Gauss–Picard–Nevanlinna background.

## Thurston as the second historical axis

- **François Laudenbach, Athanase Papadopoulos, “W. P. Thurston and French mathematics.”** arXiv:1912.03115.
  https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.03115

- **Ken'ichi Ohshika, Athanase Papadopoulos, “A glimpse into Thurston's work.”** arXiv:2009.06344, math.GT / math.HO.
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.06344

- **Athanase Papadopoulos, “Some footnotes on Thurston's Notes ‘The Geometry and Topology of 3-manifolds’.”** arXiv:2402.08302, math.GT / math.HO.
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.08302

These belong beside Nielsen–Thurston classification rather than in a separate “history” silo: the historical trail explains where hyperbolic structures, laminations, deformation spaces and geometrization entered the subject.

## Reading order for this repository

1. A'Campo–Ji–Papadopoulos on early moduli/Teichmüller history.
2. Tissot / quasiconformal-history papers.
3. Farb–Margalit Chapters 10–15.
4. Ohshika–Papadopoulos and Laudenbach–Papadopoulos on Thurston.
5. Papadopoulos's technical Thurston-metric notes.
6. Lambert/Lobachevsky/Hilbert material when the metric and hyperbolic background becomes relevant.
